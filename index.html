Reskilling in the Age of AI

Key strategies and insights to support reskilling in your team

Why reskilling matters

Companies that invest in reskilling can build a competitive edge by developing talent with skills that are scarce in the labor market.

With the half-life of skills shrinking (some tech skills last as little as 2.5 years), reskilling is essential to staying competitive.

Companies like Vodafone, Amazon, and ICICI Bank show that reskilling can create long-term employee engagement, improve retention, and fill key skill gaps.

Key responsibilities for team leads

  • Champion reskilling: team leads must take ownership of reskilling efforts, creating clear links between company goals and reskilling strategies.
  • Engage employees: foster an environment where employees understand the value of reskilling and feel supported in the process.
  • Identify Resources:be proactive in identifying internal resources, such as training programs, platforms, or mentors, that can aid in reskilling efforts.

Successful reskilling programs

  • Clear strategy alignment: reskilling efforts must align with the company’s strategic goals. For instance, Ericsson focuses on upskilling employees to transition from telecom to AI and data science.
  • Tailored approaches: employees should be involved in the creation of reskilling programs and understand how the training benefits their career paths. For example, McDonald’s employees have access to an app where the can see a direct link between skills learned and future career opportunities within the company.
  • On-the-job learning: reskilling is most effective when employees can learn on the job through shadowing or apprenticeships. For example, ICICI implements an eight-month deployment in a bank branch, where employees closely shadow a current manager to gain practical experience.

Overcoming challenges

Make reskilling an attractive and valuable proposition. Transparency, clear communication, and understanding employee motivations are key.

Involve middle managers in the design and execution of reskilling programs to ensure their teams participate and support reskilling efforts.

Companies like Bosch and Vodafone are dedicating time to employee development by allowing employees to take time off for learning or covering tuition costs.

The role of partnerships

  • Industry coalitions: companies can join forces with others in the industry to tackle shared reskilling challenges, like the Technology in Finance Immersion Programme.
  • Nonprofit collaborations: partnering with nonprofits can help organizations tap into underrepresented talent pools, providing opportunities for disadvantaged groups while meeting corporate talent needs.
  • Educational partnerships: collaborating with educational institutions can accelerate reskilling efforts and allow companies to better meet evolving technological demands.

Key concepts

⚙️
Skill taxonomy
A detailed description of the capabilities needed for each occupation at a company.
⚖️
Workplace supply & demand
Balancing the available talent pool (both internal and external) with the skills required to keep the company competitive.
🔄
Change management
The broader organizational shift that must accompany skill development initiatives, including mindset shifts and leadership alignment.
💼
Talent marketplaces
Internal platforms (often AI-powered) that match employees to projects, roles, or learning paths based on their skills and career goals.
🔒
Talent hoarding
The reluctance of managers to allow employees to reskill, fearing they will leave for other roles.
🛠️
Learning in the flow of work
The practice of integrating learning into daily tasks through job shadowing or apprenticeships rather than traditional classroom training.

Takeaway

Reskilling is no longer just a reactive measure; it's a critical part of strategic workforce planning that drives long-term organizational success.
